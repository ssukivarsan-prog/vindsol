About Heat Pump Technology

Harnessing solar thermal energy stored in ambient air to heat water efficiently without carbon emissions or ozone depletion.

THE PHYSICS OF HEAT TRANSFER

4-Stage Refrigerant Cycle

A heat pump does not generate heat by burning fuel or passing high electric current through resistive wires. Instead, it absorbs ambient solar heat from outdoor air using eco-refrigerant fluids and pumps it into water.

Because it simply moves heat rather than creating it from scratch, VINDSOL heat pumps achieve Coefficient of Performance (COP) ratings between 3.0x and 5.83x, consuming 70% to 75% less electrical power.

Stage 1: Evaporation (Air Heat Capture)

Cold liquid refrigerant flows through finned coils. Ambient air is drawn over the coil by a fan. The refrigerant absorbs heat from the air and evaporates into a low-pressure gas.

Stage 2: Compression (Pressure & Temp Boost)

The rotary or scroll compressor compresses the refrigerant gas, boosting its pressure and raising its temperature up to 85°C–90°C.

Stage 3: Condensation (Heat Exchanger)

Hot gas passes through the Tube-in-Tube or Titanium heat exchanger, transferring heat into cold water. The refrigerant condenses back into liquid.

Stage 4: Expansion (Pressure Drop)

Saginomya Electronic Expansion Valve (EEV) reduces pressure, sub-cooling liquid refrigerant to repeat the continuous thermodynamic cycle.

ENVIRONMENTAL COMMITMENT

Zero Ozone Depletion Potential (ODP)

VINDSOL exclusively uses environmentally responsible refrigerants engineered to prevent stratospheric ozone layer depletion.

R134a Refrigerant

High-efficiency non-toxic HFC refrigerant used in domestic monoblock systems. Delivers excellent high-temperature water heating stability without ozone damage.

R407c Refrigerant

Zeotropic ternary blend used in commercial heat pump systems. Ideal for retrofitting older heating systems with superior COP performance.

Near-azeotropic blend offering higher volumetric capacity and system pressure, ideal for compact commercial swimming pool heat exchangers.
